\# Business Insights and Recommendations



\## Executive Summary



The analysis identified key churn drivers across contract type, tenure, payment method, monthly charges, and support services. Customers with month-to-month contracts, low tenure, high monthly charges, and no technical support showed higher churn risk.



\## Key Insights



\### 1. Month-to-Month Contracts Have Higher Churn



Customers on month-to-month contracts are more likely to churn compared to one-year and two-year contract customers.



\### 2. Low-Tenure Customers Are More Likely to Leave



Customers within the first 12 months show higher churn risk. This indicates a need for stronger onboarding and early engagement.



\### 3. Electronic Check Users Show Higher Churn



Customers using electronic check payment method have higher churn tendency compared to automatic payment users.



\### 4. Customers Without Tech Support Are at Higher Risk



Customers without technical support show higher churn, especially among internet service users.



\### 5. High Monthly Charge Customers Create Revenue Risk



Customers paying high monthly charges represent a major revenue-risk segment if they churn.



\## Recommendations



\### 1. Convert Month-to-Month Customers to Annual Contracts



Offer discounts or loyalty benefits to encourage longer contract commitments.



\### 2. Improve First 90-Day Customer Onboarding



Provide welcome calls, service education, and early issue resolution for new customers.



\### 3. Promote Automatic Payment Methods



Encourage customers using electronic checks to shift to credit card or bank transfer auto-pay.



\### 4. Offer Free Tech Support Trial



Provide limited-time tech support access to customers without support plans.



\### 5. Prioritize High-CLV Customers



Retention teams should focus first on customers with high CLV and high churn probability.
